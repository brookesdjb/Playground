iPhone tethering: plug the iPhone into the router's USB port, unlock it, tap Trust (first time only), and turn on Settings → Personal Hotspot → Allow Others to Join. When the iPhone is connected it is used in preference to the Wi-Fi hotspot. Power the router from a 2 A+ supply — it also charges the phone.

Connect to a hotspot

NetworkSignalChSecurity

Enter the Wi-Fi MAC your laptop used on the hotspot network. Phones and laptops often use a private/random MAC per network, so read it from that network's details (Windows: Wi-Fi → hotspot → Properties → "Physical address"/"Random hardware address"; macOS: Wi-Fi → Details → "Wi-Fi address"; Linux: ip link).

How the MAC trick works
  1. Log in to the hotspot's captive portal on your laptop as normal.
  2. Note the laptop's Wi-Fi MAC for that network, then disconnect the laptop from the hotspot (and turn off auto-join) — two devices with one MAC on the same hotspot will fight.
  3. Enter the hotspot and that MAC here, press Connect. The hotspot sees the "same" device that already logged in.
  4. Plug the laptop into the router's Ethernet port (or join the setup Wi-Fi) and press "Check internet".

Alternative without cloning: leave the MAC empty, connect, note the "Uplink MAC" above, then set your laptop's MAC to that value and log in to the portal from the laptop.

Setup Wi-Fi (this router's own network)

Router address: 192.168.88.1. The setup network is switched off on whichever band the uplink uses; it stays on the other band and Ethernet always works.

Firmware

Back up first (once): the factory partition holds this unit's Wi-Fi calibration and MAC address.

Use TP-Link's 0.9.1 image for TL-WR902AC v4 (e.g. TL-WR902ACv4_EU_0.9.1_0.3_up_boot[240903-rel41878]…bin) — the newer 0.9.3 files are encrypted and are refused. The bootloader and calibration data are never touched.

Advanced: OpenWrt LuCI at /cgi-bin/luci, or SSH ssh root@192.168.88.1 — no password until you set one (passwd).
